Simple Inductive Rule for Initial Algebras

Written by: Gabriel Field.

Theorem statement

Theorem. (Simple inductive rule for proving that maps out of initial algebras are equal)

Let $T\ i \xrightarrow{\texttt{cons}} i$ be the initial algebra for an endofunctor $T : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{C}$ and let $f, g : i \to a$ be parallel arrows out of $i$. Suppose that the equaliser $ι : e \rightarrowtail i$ of $f$ and $g$ exists in $\mathsf{C}$. Then, if $f \circ \texttt{cons} \circ T\ ι = g \circ \texttt{cons} \circ T\ ι$, we must have $f = g$.

The dual result is often more useful. Let $j \xrightarrow{\texttt{des}} T\ j$ be the terminal coalgebra for $T : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{C}$ and let $f, g : a \to j$ be parallel arrows into $j$. Suppose that the coequaliser $\pi : j \twoheadrightarrow e$ of $f$ and $g$ exists in $\mathsf{C}$. Then, if $T\ \pi \circ \texttt{des} \circ f = T\ \pi \circ \texttt{des} \circ g$, we must have $f = g$.

Example Uses

Our article on coinduction has many example applications of this rule. They include reasoning about addition on the conatural numbers, various properties of functions on colists, and a proof that the "ZipList" applicative satisfies the applicative laws.
