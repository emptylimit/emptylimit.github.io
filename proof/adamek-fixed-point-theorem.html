Adámek's Fixed Point Theorem

Written by: Gabriel Field.

Theorem statement

Theorem. (Adámek's fixed point theorem)

Let $T : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{C}$ be an endofunctor. Suppose that $\mathsf{C}$ contains an initial object $0$ and the colimit of the sequence $$\begin{align*} 0 \xrightarrow{!} T\ 0 \xrightarrow{T !} T^2\ 0 \xrightarrow{T^2 !} T^3\ 0 \xrightarrow{T^3 !} \cdots \end{align*}$$ and suppose furthermore that $T$ preserves this colimit. Then, the composite isomorphism $T(\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0) \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1} 0 \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0$[1] [1] The first isomorphism is because $T$ preserves the colimit. The second isomorphism is obtained by re-indexing the term in the colimit; adding $0 \to T\ 0$ to the diagram does not impose extra requirements on the colimit cocone. gives $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0$ an algebra structure for $T$. Furthermore, $T(\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0) \simeq \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0$ is an initial algebra.

Here, we write $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i 0$ for the colimit of the above sequence. The sequence obtained by applying $T$ to every object and arrow displayed above has its colimit denoted by $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1} 0$ (and we show in our proof that this colimit exists).

The statement of this result was adapted from this nLab article.

The dual result can also be used to construct terminal coalgebras, and provides useful intuition for them.

Proof

We first argue that $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1} 0$ exists.

We wish to find the colimit of this diagram. Equivalently, we wish to characterise the cocones under it. Any cocone (drawn in solid blue) under the diagram can be uniquely extended by adding the unique arrow $\alpha_0 : 0 \to a$ (drawn in dashed magenta). Conversely, any cocone $\alpha$ under the extended diagram $0 \to T\ 0 \to \cdots$ restricts to a cocone under $T\ 0 \to \cdots$ by ignoring $\alpha_0$. That is, the cocones under the extended diagram are the same as the cocones under the diagram of interest. Since the cocones under $0 \to T\ 0 \to \cdots$ and under $T\ 0 \to \cdots$ are the same and the colimit $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$ exists, the colimit $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1}\, 0$ must exist and be are isomorphic to $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$ (via an isomorphism commuting with the colimit cocones). Alternatively, one could define $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1}\, 0 := \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$.

For simplicity, we take the approach of putting $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^{i + 1}\, 0 := \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$. We next construct an algebra structure on $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$.

Denote by $ι$ the colimit cocone under $0 \to T\ 0 \to \cdots$. Then, $T ι$ is a cocone under $T\ 0 \to T^2\ 0 \to \cdots$. Hence, there is a unique map $f$ as displayed above such that for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, the above diagram commutes. Since $T$ preserves the colimit of $0 \to T\ 0 \to \cdots$, the map $f$ is an isomorphism. Thus, $f^{-1}$ is an algebra for $T$, such that the above diagram commutes for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.

It remains to show that this is algebra is initial. Fix an algebra $T\ a \xrightarrow{\alpha} a$, and suppose we have an algebra morphism $u : \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0 \to a$ into it. We will show that $u$ is uniquely determined.

Since $u$ is an arrow out of a colimit, it is determined by its restrictions $u_i := u \circ ι_i$. Our goal will be to show that each of these restrictions is uniquely determined. We will show that $(u_i)_{i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}$ satisfies a recurrence relation, so it is uniquely determined[2] [2] I.e. by induction/recursion from $\mathbb{N}$, which is very on-brand here!. The starting restriction $u_0 : 0 \to a$ is uniquely determined in $\mathsf{C}$. We next show that $u_{i + 1}$ is uniquely determined by $u_i$. The above diagram commutes (as the reader should check). Using the trick $f^{-1} \circ f = 1$, we can see that $u_{i + 1} = \alpha \circ T\ u_i$. Thus, $u_{i + 1}$ is uniquely determined by $u_i$.

Thus, $u$ is uniquely determined, and we have a recursive formula for the restrictions $u \circ ι_i$.

It remains to actually construct an arrow $u : \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0 \to a$ which is a morphism of algebras. Being an arrow out of a colimit in $\mathsf{C}$, we can construct $u$ by specifying a cocone $(u_i : T^i\ 0 \to a)_{i \in \omega}$; our previous work suggests a recursive formula for this cocone: take $u_0 : 0 \to a$ to be the unique such arrow, and for each $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$, set $u_{i + 1} : T^{i + 1}\ 0 \to a$ to be the composite $T^{i + 1} \xrightarrow{T\ u_i} T\ a \xrightarrow{\alpha} a$. We verify that this is a genuine cocone; i.e. that $u_i = u_{i + 1} \circ T^i\ {!}$ for all $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.

We will iteratively describe $u_0, u_1, \ldots$. The first term $u_0$ is rather trivial to picture. We add $u_1 := \alpha \circ T\ u_0$ to the picture; it occupies the displayed diagonal. Since arrow out of $0$ are unique, the outer square commutes, and so $u_1 = u_0 \circ {!}$. Applying $T$ to our previous diagram, we obtain the magenta sub-diagram above (the rightmost square), which we can paste to the right of our previous diagram. Being the image of a commutative diagram under a functor, the magenta sub-diagram commutes; therefore, the whole diagram displayed above commutes. The arrow $u_2 = \alpha \circ T\ u_1$ is then given by any of the composite paths $T^2\ 0 \to \cdots \to a$ displayed above. This is easiest to see by first taking the diagonal $T\ u_1$ and then taking $\alpha$, but it will be useful to instead think of $u_2$ as the path $\alpha \circ T\ \alpha \circ T^2\ u_0$ highlighted in magenta above. Similarly, it is useful to think of $u_1$ as the path $\alpha \circ T\ u_0$ displayed above. Since the diagram commutes, it is evident that $\textcolor{blue}{u_1} = \textcolor{magenta}{u_2} \circ T\ {!}$. Extend the diagram further, adding the composite path $\textcolor{blue}{u_3} = \alpha \circ T\ \alpha \circ T^2\ \alpha \circ T^3\ {!}$ depicted above. The added square commutes because it is the image of the middle commutative square under $T$. Since the diagram commutes, it is evident that $\textcolor{magenta}{u_2} = \textcolor{blue}{u_3} \circ T^2\ {!}$. Continuing inductively, we verify that $(u_i : T^i\ 0 \to a)_{i \in \omega}$ is a cocone under $0 \to T\ 0 \to \cdots$.

The universal property of $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$ yields the desired arrow $u : \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0 \to a$ with $u_i = u \circ ι_i$ for all $i$. We next verify that $u$ is an algebra morphism.

Our goal is to show that this diagram commutes. Because $T$ preserves the colimit $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$, the cocone $(T\ ι_i)_{i \in \omega}$ is a colimit cocone. Applying the universal property, it suffices to show that $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i = \alpha \circ T\ u \circ T\ ι_i$ for each $i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$.[3] [3] One could alternatively use the universal property of $\mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$, but then a special case for $ι_0$ must be made (albeit a trivial one). The formulation I've presented here makes the most sense to me.. Fixing $i$, we can fill out the diagram as above. We will identify parts of this diagram which commute. The upper-left and upper-right triangles (displayed in magenta) commute by definition of $u$. The lower-left and lower-right triangles (displayed in blue) also commute. We saw this in our construction of the $u_i$. The upper and lower trapezoids (displayed in magenta) obviously commute. The outer square obviously commutes. To summarise, every shape in the diagram commutes, except for the middle square (displayed in red, containing the $\text{``\textcolor{red}{nope}''}$). We now chase the composite $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i$ around the diagram, displayed in blue squiggly lines. We now chase the composite $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i$ around the diagram, displayed in blue squiggly lines. We now chase the composite $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i$ around the diagram, displayed in blue squiggly lines. We now chase the composite $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i$ around the diagram, displayed in blue squiggly lines. We now chase the composite $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i$ around the diagram, displayed in blue squiggly lines. Our diagram chase shows $u \circ f^{-1} \circ T\ ι_i = \alpha \circ T\ u \circ T\ ι_i$. Since this holds for all $i$, the universal property of $T\left( \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0 \right)$ guarantees that $u \circ f^{-1} = \alpha \circ T\ u$.

Therefore, $u$ is an algebra morphism, so $T\left( \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0 \right) \xrightarrow{f^{-1}} \mathrm{colim}_{i \in \omega} T^i\, 0$ is an initial algebra for $T$. $\square$

Example Uses

One can construct initial algebras and terminal coalgebras for the following functors $T : \mathbf{Set} \to \mathbf{Set}$ this way:

  • $T : X \mapsto 1 + X$ (initial algebra: $\mathbb{N}$; terminal coalgebra: $\mathrm{co}\mathbb{N} = \mathbb{N} + \{\infty\}$)
  • $T : X \mapsto A \times X$ for fixed $A \in \mathbf{Set}$ (initial algebra: $\varnothing$; terminal coalgebra: $\texttt{Stream}\ A = A^{\mathbb{N}}$)
  • $T : X \mapsto 1 + A \times X$ for fixed $A \in \mathbf{Set}$ (initial algebra: $\texttt{List}\ A$; terminal coalgebra: $\texttt{coList}\ A = \texttt{List}\ A + \texttt{Stream}\ A$)
  • $T : X \mapsto 1 + A \times X^2$ for fixed $A \in \mathbf{Set}$ (initial algebra: finite-height binary trees over $A$; terminal coalgebra: finite-or-infinite-height binary trees over $A$)

This construction also works for $T : X \mapsto 1 + X$ regarded as a functor $T : \mathbf{Top} \to \mathbf{Top}$; the initial algebra is $\mathbb{N}$ with the discrete topology, whilst the terminal coalgebra is $\mathrm{co}\mathbb{N}$ with a topology such that $0, 1, \ldots \to \infty$[4] [4] Precisely describing this topology is a worthwhile exercise in understanding the topology on a limit in $\mathbf{Top}$..
