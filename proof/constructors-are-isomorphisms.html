Constructors are Isomorphisms

Written by: Gabriel Field.

Theorem statement

Theorem.

Let $T\ i \xrightarrow{\texttt{cons}} i$ be the initial algebra for an endofunctor $T : \mathsf{C} \to \mathsf{C}$. Then, $\texttt{cons} : T\ i \simeq i$ is an isomorphism in $\mathsf{C}$.

Proof

Observe the following.

$T\ \texttt{cons}$ is an algebra homomorphism and $\texttt{cons}$ is an initial algebra, so there exists a unique algebra morphism $d$ as displayed above. We will show that $d = \texttt{cons}^{-1}$. $\texttt{cons}$ itself is an algebra homomorphism from $T^2\ i \xrightarrow{T\ \texttt{cons}} T\ i$ to $T\ i \xrightarrow{\texttt{cons}} i$, so the bottom square commutes. The whole diagram thus commutes, showing that $\texttt{cons} \circ d$ is an algebra morphism. Since $T\ i \xrightarrow{\texttt{cons}} i$ is initial and $1_i$ is also an algebra morphism to $T\ i \xrightarrow{\texttt{cons}} i$, we have $1_i = \texttt{cons} \circ d$. It remains to show that $d \circ \texttt{cons} = 1_{T\ i}$. The outer square commutes since $d$ is an algebra morphism, so the map along the diagonal is uniquely determined. Taking the left-bottom composite and knowing already that $\texttt{cons} \circ d = 1_i$, we see that the diagonal map is $1_{T\ i}$. Comparing with the other composite, we get our desired equation $d \circ \texttt{cons} = 1_{T\ i}$.

Therefore, $\texttt{cons}$ is an isomorphism in $\mathsf{C}$. $\square$
