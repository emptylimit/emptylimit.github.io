Initial Objects Have No Proper Subobjects

Written by: Gabriel Field.

Theorem statement

Theorem.

Let $i$ be the initial object in a category $\mathsf{C}$, and let $ι : a \rightarrowtail i$ be a monomorphism into $i$. Then, $ι$ is an isomorphism in $\mathsf{C}$.

Proof

Observe the following.

Since $i$ is initial, there is a map $f : i \to a$. We will show that $f = ι^{-1}$. Since $i$ is initial, uniqueness of maps $i \to i$ forces $ι \circ f = 1_i$. It remains to show $f \circ ι = 1_a$. The outer square and bottom-right triangle commute. Chasing through, we see that $ι \circ f \circ ι = ι \circ 1_a$. Since $ι$ is a monomorphism, this implies $f \circ ι = 1_a$.

Therefore, $ι$ is an isomorphism with inverse $f$. $\square$
